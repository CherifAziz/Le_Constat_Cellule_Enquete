8. FRANCE 
 
122 
Rising energy prices and fiscal adjustment weigh on demand  
Real GDP growth is expected to decrease to 0.4% 
in 2026. In the first three quarters of 2026, 
economic activity was dragged down by a drop in 
residential investment, following the suspension 
of subsidies for renovation, and weaker public 
investment. In addition, exports were hit by supply 
bottlenecks in aeronautics in 2026-Q3. Still, net 
exports are projected to contribute 0.1 pps. to 
GDP growth in 2026. Higher energy prices and 
headline inflation are weighing on private 
consumption, which is expected to remain 
subdued over the year, despite a small decline in 
the saving rate to a still high rate of 17.3%. On 
the production side, lower agricultural output due 
to the summer exceptional heatwaves and 
drought also detracted from growth. 
In 2027, economic activity is projected to gain some momentum, despite the fiscal consolidation 
included in the draft budget. Real GDP growth is set to reach 0.6%. This improvement is supported 
by the dissipation of the temporary factors weighing on activity in 2026. Private consumption is 
expected to pick up and to contribute 0.3 pps. to GDP growth, leading to a further projected decline 
of the saving rate to 16.6%. Investment is set to detract 0.2 pps. from GDP growth, weighed down 
by residential and equipment investment. Net exports are expected to accelerate, driven by 
aeronautics and defence, as well as by accelerating economic activity in France’s trade partners. 
Unemployment set to increase 
The labour market weakened in 2026, with the unemployment rate rising to 8.3% in 2026-Q2, up 
0.7 pps. y-o-y, against a backdrop of an increased labour force. The activity rate of people aged 15 
to 64 was stable in 2026-Q2 at a record high of 75.4%. The labour force is projected to increase in 
2027 and to accelerate in 2028, following the assumed reinstatement of the 2023 pension 
reform. Payroll employment is expected to decline by 0.3% in 2026 and 0.5% in 2027, before 
picking up again in 2028 (+0.1%). The unemployment rate is set to increase from 8.4% in 2026 to 
9.2% in 2027 and 9.8% in 2028.  
Inflation expected to increase due to higher energy and food prices 
Inflation rose to 3.8% in October 2026, reflecting a 24.1% surge in energy prices and a 2.7% 
increase in services prices. Inflation is expected to peak at 4.3% in 2027-Q1 due to further 
increases in energy prices and their pass-through to food and services prices. It is then set to 
decline to 1.6% in 2027-Q4 as energy prices gradually decline. Headline inflation is projected at 
2.9% on average in 2027 and at 2.1% in 2028. 
Economic growth is set to decelerate to 0.4% in 2026, dragged down by weak investment, high 
energy prices and the adverse impact of the summer’s heatwaves. It is expected to increase to 
0.6% in 2027, driven by net exports and a rebound in agricultural production. In 2028, in the 
absence of further adjustment, economic activity is forecast to accelerate to 1.4%. High energy 
prices are set to lift inflation to 2.6% in 2026 and 2.9% in 2027. The government deficit is 
expected at 5.5% in 2026, before declining to 5.4% in 2027 and edging up again in 2028 under a 
no-policy-change assumption. Public debt is set to increase from 115.7% of GDP in 2025 to 
123.2% by 2028, driven by continuing sizeable deficits.

Euro Area Member States, France 
 
123 
Large primary deficits and rising interest payments leading to higher public debt  
The general government deficit is expected to rise to 5.5% of GDP in 2026, from 5.1% in 2025. 
This increase reflects the slowdown in economic activity and associated sluggish tax revenue, as 
well as a 0.4 pps. increase —to 2.6% of GDP —of interest payments on government debt. These 
factors are expected to more than offset the impact of the deficit-reducing measures implemented 
since spring, namely expenditure savings across ministries and public administrations of about 
0.3% of GDP and, on the revenue side, the freezing of social contribution exemptions. 
In 2027, the general government deficit is expected to decline only slightly to 5.4% of GDP, as the 
projected muted economic growth and increasing interest payments on public debt offset the 
significant consolidation effort embedded in the forecast. On the expenditure side, the announced 
measures in the draft budget, which underpin the forecast, are worth about 0.8% of GDP. They 
include the partial freezing of pensions, the freezing and reform of several social benefits and 
across-the-board efforts to contain expenditure at various levels of public administration. At the 
same time, interest payments are projected to increase by 0.4 pps., to 3% of GDP, driven by higher 
interest rates on new bond issuances and payments on inflation-indexed bonds. On the revenue 
side, while tax receipts are still expected to grow broadly in line with economic activity, newly-
announced and past revenue-increasing measures account for about 0.7% of GDP. They include, 
among others, the prolongation of the exceptional contribution by large enterprises, the renewed 
freezing of social contributions’ exemptions, the capping or reduction of tax exemptions, including 
for pensioners and on work-related social benefits, and an exceptional levy on dormant assets. 
In 2028, under a no-policy-change assumption, the general government deficit is expected to 
increase to 5.7% of GDP. While some revenue measures planned for 2027 are set to expire, tax 
revenue is overall projected to evolve broadly in line with economic activity. On the expenditure 
side, interest payments are expected to increase further, to 3.3% of GDP.  
The government debt-to-GDP ratio is projected to remain on an upward trend over the forecast 
horizon and to exceed 123% in 2028. This trend, assuming unchanged policies, is set to be mainly 
driven by high primary deficits and rising interest payments.

9. CROATIA 
 
124 
Economic growth to moderate in 2026 and pick up thereafter  
Croatia’s real GDP growth is forecast to moderate from 3.5% in 2025 to 2.1% in 2026, before 
picking up to 2.4% in 2027 and maintaining a similar, albeit marginally lower, pace in 2028. 
Growth is expected to remain above the EU average throughout the forecast horizon, driven mainly 
by domestic demand, while the negative contribution of net exports is projected to diminish. 
Private consumption growth is expected to 
moderate in 2026, despite continued growth in 
real wages and employment, as households 
remain cautious about spending decisions due to 
persistent price pressures. Investment growth is 
expected to slow markedly, as rising costs, tighter 
financing conditions and heightened uncertainty 
weigh on firms’ investment decisions. The 
absorption of EU funds, particularly during the 
final phase of RRF implementation, is expected to 
provide some support to investment. Goods 
exports are forecast to expand steadily, albeit at 
a slower pace than in 2025, supported by 
demand from the main trading partners. By 
contrast, following two years of contraction, 
services exports are projected to grow modestly, as persistent price and cost competitiveness 
pressures, particularly in tourism, continue to constrain growth. Tourism activity in 2026 has 
remained comparable to 2025 but is less buoyant than during the post-pandemic recovery. Import 
growth is expected to moderate, reflecting slower domestic demand growth. As a result, net 
exports are projected to make a negative contribution to GDP growth in 2026, though less than in 
2025. In 2027 and 2028, economic activity is expected to expand steadily at 2.4% and 2.3%, 
respectively. Private consumption is set to grow above 2% in both years, in line with real 
disposable income growth. Investment growth is expected to rise in 2027, on the back of stronger 
activity in the second half of 2026, and to remain solid in 2028, as cost pressures ease, while the 
phase out of the RRF is expected to be partly offset by a pick-up in the absorption of other EU 
funds. Favourable external demand is set to support stronger growth in goods and services exports. 
Imports are forecast to continue to grow in line with domestic demand. The negative contribution 
of net exports to GDP growth is expected to diminish further. The current account deficit is 
projected to widen to 5.2% of GDP in 2027 and remain stable in 2028, reflecting a persistent 
goods trade deficit partly offset by the services surplus. Net current and capital transfers are 
expected to continue supporting Croatia’s external position, although their contribution is set to 
edge down gradually over the forecast horizon. 
Contained employment growth and weakening wage pressures 
Employment growth is set to continue but to remain below 1% over the forecast horizon as 
economic activity moderates. Labour shortages, which remained elevated in recent years, are 
expected to ease, due to lower labour demand and continued inflows of non-EU workers. At the 
Croatia’s GDP growth is expected to moderate in 2026 to 2.1%, before expanding steadily at 
2.4% and 2.3% in 2027 and 2028, remaining above the EU average. Employment growth is set to 
continue but to remain below 1% over the forecast horizon, with the unemployment rate staying 
at historically low levels. Inflation is expected to stay elevated at 4.2% in 2026, before easing to 
2.6% in 2028, as services and energy inflation decreases. The general government deficit is 
expected to remain close to 3% for the forecast horizon, while the decline in the debt to GDP ratio 
is foreseen to stop.
